I work in software quality and test automation, but the part that interests me most is not the tooling. It is understanding why things fail and how we can build systems and teams that handle complexity better.

Software is a puzzle. So are the processes around it, and so are the teams building it. My work is about finding the hidden problems: the assumptions nobody questioned, the complexity nobody removed, and the weak points that only become visible when things start breaking.

I care about practical quality, not quality theatre. The goal is not more checklists, more meetings, or more automation for the sake of automation. The goal is giving people confidence: confidence to change software, confidence to release it, and confidence that the systems they build will behave the way they expect.

I believe good engineering creates space for people to do better work. The right processes, tools, and practices should remove friction rather than create it. If a process needs three meetings to explain itself, it is probably the process that needs testing.

A big part of my work is sharing what I learn. Through writing, speaking, and conversations with others, I try to make ideas easier to apply, mistakes easier to avoid, and teams better equipped to solve their own problems.

Because in the end, quality is not something you add to software. It is something you build into the way people think.
